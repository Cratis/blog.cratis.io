Ten posts on keeping a decision true at the moment it's written, and one page that tells you which mechanism a rule needs and where it's explained.

The question I'd ask first about any rule is not "which tool?" but "what shape is it?". A unique email is a constraint, because no read of one key can see another key. A document two people edit needs the revision the user saw, not a fresh read at save time. A rule you'd rather repair later needs no guard at all. State on a key you read is where Arc's read protection fits.

For each shape the guide names what to use, what to never rely on, and what is too much. "Never rely on" is the column that matters: those are the choices where the command succeeds, the tests pass one request at a time, and the rule is broken in production.

In the post:
- a decision guide from rule shape to mechanism to the part that explains it
- 22 short answers: retries, refusals, what a guard doesn't cover, the caller's severity threshold, Measure, cost, other languages, DCB, testing
- the full series index and the docs to go to next
