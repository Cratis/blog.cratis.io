Model-bound projections get most of the attention in Chronicle, and I get why. You put an attribute on a property and the read model says where its data comes from.

But the fluent, declarative IProjectionFor<T> was the first thing we built, and I think it's undersold today.

The trade-off model-bound makes is real: the read model, the events it listens to, the keys, the children and the removals all end up in the same place. Fluent keeps a plain record on one side and the mapping on the other. Same engine, same projection definition stored in the kernel. Different trade-off.

And then there's the thing people mix it up with: the reducer. It's not a third spelling of the same idea. A reducer is your code, running in your application, taking an event and the current state and returning the next state.

In the post I go through:
- the same book example, written both ways, in C#, Kotlin, Java, TypeScript and Elixir
- when fluent earns its keep (including Kotlin and Java, where it can express things the annotations can't)
- how projections differ from reducers, and how I'd choose between the three
- how to specify all of it with ReadModelScenario

My rule of thumb: start model-bound, reach for fluent when the attributes stop being readable, and reach for a reducer when the logic reads better as code.
